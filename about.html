Who CBETA is

CBETA — the Chinese Buddhist Electronic Text Association, formally the 財團法人佛教電子佛典基金會 — is a nonprofit foundation based in Taipei. Since 1998 it has built and maintained the largest digitised collection of Chinese Buddhist texts in existence, and given all of it away for free.

How it started

CBETA was founded on 15 February 1998 by the Buddhist studies scholars Ven. Heng-ching (National Taiwan University) and Ven. Hui-min (then at the National Institute of the Arts), with early funding from the Yin-Shun Foundation of North America and the Chung-Hwa Institute of Buddhist Studies. Its founding decision shaped everything that followed: rather than re-keying the canon from scratch, CBETA set out to gather texts that had already been digitised elsewhere and cross-check them against the print editions — collation, the same discipline the original Taishō editors used on the manuscripts a century earlier. The first six volumes shipped on CD-ROM and online in December 1998, ten months after the foundation existed at all.

What it grew into

Nearly thirty years on, CBETA is the reference digital text for Chinese Buddhist studies worldwide — used, by its own description, by "academic researchers, practitioners, and Buddhist culture enthusiasts" on every continent. An advisory committee formed in 2024 now reviews proposed additions, and CBETA has published its own source code and data publicly on GitHub, a step further into the open than most textual-heritage projects take.

Why the site still reads like 1998

CBETA's growth has been almost entirely in the collection and the scholarship around it — not in explaining itself to a newcomer. Its own homepage is written for people who already know what they're looking for: six navigation menus, each with up to nine sub-items, no plain-language "what is this" for a first-time reader, and no English beyond a donation form. That's not a criticism of the archive, which is the actual achievement — it's the reason this page exists instead of just linking straight through.
