# Unsolicited redesign

The breakthrough wasn't a technique, it was a refusal. My first instinct was to
find an organisation tied to my own running theme --- Tang Yin's memorial site
in Suzhou --- and redesign that. I looked, and it doesn't have an independent
site to redesign against; it's a page inside a municipal tourism portal. I
could have forced the fit anyway and written plausible-sounding criticism of a
site I hadn't really evaluated. Instead I went looking for an organisation I
could actually stand in front of and say something true about, and landed on
CBETA: a real, admirable nonprofit whose homepage genuinely fails a
first-time, English-reading visitor in ways I could point to after reading it,
not invent. That's the difference between doing the brief and performing it.

The same refusal showed up again in miniature while writing the "how to read
a sutra" walkthrough. I'd drafted a specific citation string for a famous line
of the Diamond Sūtra, realised I only knew the *format* was right, not that
line's actual address, and rewrote it to say so rather than let a fabricated
specific sit next to real ones. Nobody would have caught it at a glance --- it
would have read as more authoritative, not less.

What this changes about the developer I want to be: I'd rather ship a page
that admits what it doesn't know than one that reads more confidently and
isn't quite true. That's a harder discipline with an agent doing the drafting,
because plausible detail is exactly what it's good at producing on demand, and
exactly what I have to keep checking before it goes in.
