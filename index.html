The canon is enormous. The front door isn't.

CBETA, the Chinese Buddhist Electronic Text Association, has spent almost thirty years turning the Chinese Buddhist canon — thousands of scrolls, tens of thousands of pages — into a searchable digital archive that scholars everywhere now treat as the default. It is one of the great quiet achievements of the digital humanities. Its homepage does not tell you any of this.

The collection, at a glance

Five layers, one archive

"The Buddhist canon" undersells it: CBETA stacks the Xuzangjing, the Jiaxing Canon, stone-carved sutra rubbings and modern scholarship on top of the Taishō Tripiṭaka, the standard reference edition scholars have used since the 1920s and 30s.

Then two more things worth knowing

You can read it two ways

A browser tool for searching and citing, or a desktop app for reading offline. Neither assumes you already know the citation system. See how to read a sutra.

It runs on donations and volunteers

Money keeps digitisation staff paid; code contributions on GitHub keep the tools improving. See support & contact.
